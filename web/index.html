    Nose breathing experimental

    Nostril ratio NAR = nostril width ÷ nose length. With your mouth closed, breathe through your nose and watch the bar: if nostril flare is detectable, it should oscillate with each breath. The motion is small and noisy, so this may or may not resolve a usable signal for your face/camera — this bar lets you check.

    The bar auto-scales to the recent min/max (~24 s), so even tiny changes are amplified. A rising “recent range” with a bar that pulses in time with your breathing is the signal you’re looking for; a bar that only jerks when you move your head is not.

    Talking detection experimental

    Ignores frames while you're talking (rapid mouth movement) so speech isn't counted as breathing. Vision-only — it can occasionally react to chewing/laughing. Talk normally and watch the state flip to TALKING; adjust sensitivity so speech is caught but breathing isn't.

    What this measures

    The app watches your webcam, finds your face with the MediaPipe FaceLandmarker model (478 landmarks), and computes the Mouth Aspect Ratio (MAR) — the vertical lip opening divided by the mouth width — to decide whether your lips are open or closed. Everything runs on your device; only small metadata is uploaded.

    Key terms

    MAR (Mouth Aspect Ratio)
    Mean inner-lip vertical opening ÷ inner mouth width. Higher = more open.
    Open / Close threshold (hysteresis)
    Two separate MAR levels. Once open, the mouth must fall below the lower close threshold to flip back — this stops flicker.
    Minimum duration
    A change must hold this long before it's confirmed, so brief flickers (a swallow, one noisy frame) don't count.
    Face rate
    Fraction of sampled frames where a usable face was found.
    % open
    Share of measured time your mouth was open — the headline metric tracked across days.

    How it maps to the pipeline

    Capture → foundation model (landmarks) → MAR → smoothing → stats → central store. The capture + model live in one swappable FaceSensor module; the math is ported directly from the desktop app's pure-logic core.
